# PhotoFindry 0.3.0-beta.13 · build 151

This Mac release makes removing a photo source quick and reliable, lets household tagging keep going when computers of different speeds help, and stops listing rejected review answers as problems. Mac build 151 was published on 28 November 2026. Windows keeps 0.3.0-beta.11 / build 149 and Linux keeps 0.3.0-beta.8 / build 142 on their own signed feeds. Build 150 was a private test build and was not published.

**Removing a source.** **Remove source…** now asks whether to keep a restorable archive first; with more than one source, you choose the source before that.

- **Archive, then remove** goes straight to choosing where to save the archive. It verifies the saved archive, then takes the source's photographs out of the gallery. The source stays in Files as archived, so you can attach it again later. If processing or Local AI Search is running, pause it first.
- **Remove without archive** asks for one final confirmation that the removal cannot be undone. It then permanently removes the source from PhotoFindry in one job you can follow in Jobs: its photographs and their entries in Removed, their tags, their People faces (and People who appear only in this source) and unshared cached previews.

Neither path needs a Library cleanup review or a folder check first. Original photographs in the folder are never changed or deleted. Other sources keep their photographs, tags and your own People labels. Without an archive, faces in other sources that were named automatically from the removed source's faces go back to review; with an archive, those faces keep their names, but the archived source's faces no longer name new ones. This also fixes removal failing on large libraries with "Source People metadata exceeds its storage bound."

**Household tagging with several computers (members).** A household batch can now run to the end of a 32-photo turn instead of a few photos, so computers wait for each other far less often. Tagging starts on this computer without waiting for helpers; each helper joins the running batch once its connection is ready and keeps it for the turn. Once a helper has finished a photo in the job, it stops taking new photos in a batch when, at its measured speed, its next photo would finish after the other computers had done everything left. A slow laptop therefore rarely holds up a fast PC; a photo it has already started still finishes, and this computer always keeps tagging. Turning a paired helper off in **Settings → Household tagging** stops it from starting new photos within about two seconds. A helper whose connection or model fails, or whose photo passes the three-minute response limit, gets no more photos in that batch, and a photo it could not finish is tagged on this computer. While a tagging job runs with helpers, the tagging strip shows a row for this computer and one for each helper, with its state, its photos per minute once measured and its tagged and failed counts. Helpers on build 149, including Windows, keep working with this library.

**Quieter photo review.** With the default **Current tagging model** reviewer, when an automatic photo-check answer includes wording the pixels cannot establish, such as a count of people, a name, a safety rating, an exact age or details outside a close-up crop, PhotoFindry asks the model once to correct it. If the wording remains, those statements are left out, that check is recorded as unclear and the rest of the review continues. Such an answer never marks an existing saved detail as uncertain or conflicting. A review answer that is still rejected after its one correction keeps your saved tags and is no longer listed in Needs attention, counted as a job failure or shown as a warning in photo details.

**Using more tagging slots.** After **Check performance** suggests tagging more than one photo at a time, a tagging job that is already running keeps the count it started with. Settings now says so: stop the job, then start tagging again (for example with **Tag pending**) to use the new count. Saved tags are kept.

Updates preserve the library, originals, model paths, saved lenses and saved work. All inference stays on your computers; a household helper receives only the prepared preview of each photo it tags, over an encrypted connection between paired computers. The MIT client excludes proprietary engine source and private development history. The beta 16 Mac-app migration channel is unchanged.

See QUALIFICATION.md for the exact candidate evidence and hardware limits. Public delivery is complete only after the delivered-byte, live-feed, installer, recovery and website checks pass.
